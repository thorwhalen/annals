annals.references#

The references of documents, extracted and combined, in three forms.

A document’s references are the body of its references section: a markdown heading named “References”, “Sources”, “Bibliography”, “Works cited” or “Citations” (any level, any case), down to the next heading of the same or a higher level. Three forms, each text in, text out:

  • grouped (the default): one ### section per document holding its references as they are, under ## References for <title>;

  • deduped: every entry once, its citation number removed, as one bulleted list;

  • urls: every web address once, one per line and nothing else, the form a notebook (Gemini Notebook, formerly NotebookLM) accepts as pasted website sources.

>>> doc = '''# Bees
... Text [1].
... ## REFERENCES
... 1. [Hive life](https://bees.org/hive). 2024.
... [2] Smith J. Pollen, <https://pollen.net/a>
... ## Appendix
... not a reference https://no.org
... '''
>>> reference_sections(doc)
['1. [Hive life](https://bees.org/hive). 2024.\n[2] Smith J. Pollen, <https://pollen.net/a>']
>>> [strip_citation_number(e) for e in reference_entries(reference_sections(doc)[0])]
['[Hive life](https://bees.org/hive). 2024.', 'Smith J. Pollen, <https://pollen.net/a>']
>>> print(combine_references([("Bees", [doc]), ("Wasps", ["no references here"])],
...                          title="Insects", form="urls"), end="")
https://bees.org/hive
https://pollen.net/a

The mechanism knows nothing of the store: doc_reference_texts() is the bridge that reads a set of documents through a read(doc_id, rel) callable, so the owner’s API and, later, a share can use it alike.
